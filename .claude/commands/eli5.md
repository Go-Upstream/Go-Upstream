description: Explain a topic in plain language, no jargon
---
Explain the following in plain language, as if to a smart person
with zero background in the field: $ARGUMENTS

Rules: no jargon without defining it, use a concrete analogy,
keep it under 300 words, end with the one thing that actually matters.

## Then: offer a diagram, if there is a mechanism

After the explanation, ask whether the topic contains something a picture
would show better than the prose just did. It qualifies if there is:

- two things compared, where the difference itself is the point
- a before and after, or a state that changes
- something that flows, moves, or passes between parties
- a structure the reader would otherwise have to assemble in their head

If it qualifies, offer a diagrammed page in **one line**, naming what the
diagram would show. Don't pitch it at length, and don't build it unasked.

If it doesn't — a definition, a date, a single fact, a matter of taste —
say nothing and stop at the explanation. Most topics don't qualify.

## If they accept

Load `artifact-design` and `artifact-diagramming` first, then:

- Draw the mechanism, not its name. Boxes with nouns in them are the prose
  again, in worse typography. Label the arrows.
- Comparing options? Draw the one thing that differs between them, with
  everything else held visibly identical.
- Give the page a visual identity specific to the subject. Match it to this
  repo's ink-and-gold only when the subject is Go Upstream's own — branding
  an unrelated topic implies a company position on it.
- Reserve one colour for one meaning per figure, and say in the caption what
  that figure claims.
