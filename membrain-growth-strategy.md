# Membrain — Growth Strategy Brainstorm

*Prepared for George Brontén (majority owner) · July 2026 · Working draft for discussion with Henrik*

---

## 1. Where the company actually stands

Facts on the table, stated plainly:

- ~200 customers after 14 years, concentrated in English-speaking markets plus Sweden.
- 28 people (17 SE, 10 US, 1 IE), not profitable.
- Pricing roughly $49–89/user/month — between HubSpot and Salesforce, but far below the value of embedded methodology (Salesforce needs $165+/user plus add-ons to match guided selling).
- Strong product reputation in the niche: G2 4.6/5, repeatedly named top sales CRM / sales enablement tool, praised specifically for process guidance and support.
- Positioning has cycled through four category labels in a crowded market.
- AI is a live threat: customers who use only a fraction of the platform are starting to build their own tooling.

The honest read: Membrain has won its reputation but not its market. Fourteen years in, ~200 customers and no profit means the current motion — whatever its quality — does not compound. A growth plan that is "do the same, but harder" is not a plan. The brainstorm below is organized around the few decisions that would actually change the trajectory.

---

## 2. The owner-level decision that comes before any tactic

As majority owner who is no longer operational, your first decision is not marketing — it is which game Membrain is playing. Three coherent paths:

**Path A — Profitable niche leader.** Accept that this is a niche (complex B2B sales, mid-market) and dominate it profitably. Cut to break-even fast, grow through partners and expansion revenue, become the default platform of the sales-effectiveness world. Compounds slowly but removes fragility, and a profitable niche SaaS with high NRR is sellable at any time.

**Path B — Raise and swing.** Take outside capital to buy growth in a crowded market against HubSpot's distribution and Salesforce's ecosystem. Given 14 years of evidence about the niche's size and sales cycle, this is the weakest option — the market has already told you how fast it lets you grow.

**Path C — Strategic combination.** Merge with or sell to someone who needs what Membrain has: a sales-training conglomerate wanting a software layer, a PE roll-up of sales-tech assets, or a mid-market CRM wanting a complex-sales tier.

**Recommendation: Path A, executed so that Path C stays available.** Unprofitable + AI uncertainty is the fragile combination; profitability is the strategic move, not just the financial one. Everything below assumes Path A.

## 3. Positioning: stop naming categories, start naming the enemy

Four category labels in fourteen years ("Active Pipeline Management," "Sales Effectiveness Platform," "Sales Enablement CRM," "B2B Growth Platform") is the tell. Category creation is a venture-scale marketing spend; Membrain cannot win a category war and doesn't need to. "B2B Growth Platform" is the weakest of the four — every vendor claims growth, and it says nothing about who it's for.

What has always been true, and what customers and G2 reviewers actually buy, is the *thesis*: **how you sell is why you win**. The positioning that matches it is not a category but a who/what statement with an enemy:

> *For teams selling complex B2B deals — long cycles, many stakeholders — Membrain turns your sales methodology into daily behavior. CRMs record what happened; Membrain drives what happens next.*

The enemy is the administrative CRM ("a database your reps resent updating"). The differentiator is methodology-made-operational. That is concrete, defensible, and — critically for §5 — it is the one thing generic AI does *not* commoditize.

Practical moves:

- Freeze the label debate. Pick "the sales execution platform for complex B2B sales" (descriptor, not category) and leave it alone for five years.
- Tighten the ICP in writing: 10–100 salespeople, sales cycle 3+ months, 5+ stakeholders per deal, and — the strongest signal — **already investing in a sales methodology or training partner**. That last criterion is the qualifying question that filters out everyone HubSpot serves better.
- Rewrite the comparison pages around the enemy, not feature grids: "Salesforce needs $165+/user and add-ons to enforce a sales process; Membrain ships it at $69."

## 4. Channels: what fourteen years of partner data actually says

*(Revised after reviewing the real numbers: 119 partners globally, most of whom have never referred a single client. Years of effort invested. The best partners are tech-savvy firms who want to scale and ask how Membrain fits that; the majority are lifestyle consultancies billing substantial service fees who shy away from technology. The 10–30% license commission does not motivate them.)*

That record is not an underperforming channel — it is a **disproven mechanism**, and the strategy has to say so. The economics never worked from the partner's side: a boutique trainer with two Membrain-fit clients at 20 seats each refers ~$33k ARR and earns ~$6.6k/year at 20% — less than two workshop days — while carrying asymmetric risk (a bad software rollout damages the client relationship their livelihood depends on, and behavior-tracking software can just as easily reveal that their training *didn't* stick). A rational lifestyle consultant does exactly what most of the 119 have done: nothing. No commission percentage fixes this; license commission is the wrong currency entirely.

The rework — stop running one partner program, run two different things:

**Lessons from the methodology-edition experiment.** Membrain has already tried productizing partner IP as branded "methodology editions" (e.g., the Baseline Selling edition): significant joint build time, no market interest. The post-mortem matters because the failure was structural, not executional. (1) Demand for a methodology-by-name doesn't exist at the software layer — the addressable market of each edition is that trainer's active client base, people the trainer would have brought anyway; a landing page cannot create demand for a personal brand it doesn't own. (2) A pre-packaged edition is the wrong artifact for complex-sales buyers, whose whole reason for choosing Membrain is getting *their* process operationalized. (3) Months of bespoke build per edition against one consultancy's worth of distribution is upside-down economics. The editions were the right asset monetized at the wrong layer with the wrong distribution — the corrections are below.

- **Certified tier: a concentrated bet on ~10–15 firms, invite-only.** The profile is known from the partners who actually work: tech-savvy, growth-minded, motivated by stickiness / behavior enforcement / embedding their content — not commissions — anti-mainstream-vendor by temperament, and willing to co-sell (they push back when a prospect says "we already have a CRM"). That profile is maybe 5–10% of the sales-training population, so the tier isn't "the best of the current 119" — it is *everyone worldwide who fits*, and recruiting the next ten of them is a named job. What the tier gets:
  - **A methodology encoding toolkit, not bespoke editions.** One AI-powered capability replacing N hand-built editions: the partner feeds in playbooks, decks, videos, call recordings; AI drafts the process stages, milestones, qualification criteria, scorecards, and coaching prompts in Membrain; the partner reviews and adjusts. Months of joint engineering becomes days of partner self-service — the cost asymmetry that killed the editions disappears, and "embedding content," the thing the best partners actually want, becomes routine per-engagement work instead of a special project.
  - **The encoded methodology's real job is fueling the AI coach** (§5) — automated deal reviews, between-session reinforcement, and coaching prompts grounded in *the partner's* framework. Worth little as a SKU; worth a lot as the configuration layer that makes behavior stick. This is where the partner strategy and the AI strategy become one system.
  - **A partner console** — one multi-client view to run their programs, coach, and benchmark across every client on Membrain. Membrain becomes how they scale delivery, so each new client of theirs lands on the platform by default.
  - **Retention proof for *their* contracts** — reporting framed as "your program is still driving behavior at this client eight months on," i.e., the evidence that gets the *partner's* renewal signed.
- **Fix the front door: application, not enrollment.** The current partner page (membrain.com/partner) has the right message — "87% of training is forgotten within a month without reinforcement" is exactly what the best partners buy — but the wrong funnel: "Sign up today!" open enrollment is the machine that built 119 mostly-dormant partnerships, optimizing for count over fit. And its Referral → Certified → Impact ladder assumes partners climb; the data says good ones *arrive* fitting the profile. Replace open enrollment with application against a published bar (active clients to bring onto Membrain, co-sell commitment, comfort embedding IP in the platform). Scarcity is an asset with this species of partner: being one of ~15 certified partners globally is a status good in a way that being one of 5,000 HubSpot partners cannot be — showcase the certified few by name with client results as proof of selectivity. Retire the Impact tier's "productization of custom methodologies for global scaling" promise — the edition experiment's ghost — in favor of the encoding-toolkit-plus-coach mechanism.
- **Distribution unit: the engagement, not the landing page.** Membrain is bundled into the certified partner's program by default — "the program includes the platform that makes it stick," with first-year seats folded into or co-sold alongside the program fee. Program lifecycle mirrors observed best-partner behavior: co-sell in year one, embed by default thereafter. Existing methodology landing pages get demoted to co-marketing/SEO assets aimed at the partner's own audience, with zero expectation of cold demand.
- **Everyone else: community, not channel.** Directory listing, co-marketing, podcast/webinar slots, an affiliate link — and zero partner-manager time. Lifestyle trainers will do visibility-for-free collaboration even though they will never sell. Reclaim the management cost and put it into the direct motion.
- **Recruit a different species going forward.** Boutique trainers have few clients and no tech appetite. Two adjacent types are structurally better: **fractional sales-leader networks** (tech-savvy, operationally embedded, and they choose the stack for many clients at once) and **assessment firms** (an OMG-style evaluation manufactures the "our process is broken" moment Membrain converts — trigger events at scale on someone else's marketing budget).

**The strategic goal survives; the proxy doesn't.** The insight behind the partner thesis was always the ICP signal: companies actively investing in how they sell. If partners won't transmit, go direct at the same trigger events — new VP of Sales hired, training engagement started, methodology rollout, sales-kickoff season — through assets Membrain controls: ten years of blog equity, *Stop Killing Deals*, a practitioner community, the annual benchmark report, and targeted outbound. George as evangelist-in-chief, Henrik as operator. This promotes the founder-brand/community motion from "air cover" to **primary acquisition engine**, with the certified-partner bet as a concentrated secondary.

Geographic note: this also changes the expansion logic. Partners were meant to be the zero-cost geographic strategy; with a mostly-dormant network, new regions should wait until the direct motion and the certified tier prove out in existing markets.

## 5. AI: turn the build-your-own threat into the wedge

The observed threat — lightly-engaged customers building their own apps — is a symptom worth reading precisely: the platform's value isn't reaching them where they work, and generic AI has made "good enough" tooling cheap. Two responses fail: ignoring it, and bolting on generic AI features (summaries, email drafting) that HubSpot/Salesforce ship faster and cheaper.

The response that fits Membrain's thesis:

1. **The AI sales coach, not the AI scribe.** Everyone's AI writes emails. Membrain's AI should do what only Membrain can: coach against the customer's *own encoded methodology* and deal history. Automated deal reviews ("this deal is missing an economic buyer; your process says..."), pipeline-risk calls a sales manager would make, coaching prompts for 1:1s. This is defensible because it requires the methodology + process + stakeholder data that lives only in Membrain.
2. **Become the platform agents talk to, not the one they route around.** Ship a first-class API and an MCP server so customers' own AI agents and internal apps read/write Membrain instead of replacing it. If customers are going to build, make Membrain the system of record and judgment they build *on*. Charge for it at the platform tier.
3. **The data moat.** Benchmark data across customers and engagements (the "State of B2B Sales" asset) becomes more valuable as AI needs grounding. Annual benchmark report → marketing; benchmarks inside the product ("your win rate at this stage vs. peer median") → retention.
4. **The narrative.** AI makes average selling cheap and ubiquitous, which means differentiation shifts entirely to *how you sell* — Membrain's 14-year thesis, suddenly current. That is the marketing story for the next three years, and it's yours to tell before someone else does.

Kill or don't start: any AI feature a horizontal CRM will ship as a checkbox.

## 6. The money: expansion, pricing, and the path to profit

With 200 customers, growth math favors the installed base before new logos:

- **Instrument the basics first.** NRR, gross churn, expansion rate, CAC by channel, and module penetration (Prospecting / Active Pipeline / Account Growth / Elevate) per account. If NRR isn't ≥100%, fixing that outranks all acquisition work — a leaky base makes every marketing dollar worthless.
- **Cross-sell the suite.** Four products and (likely) low multi-module penetration is the cheapest revenue available. A named expansion motion — even 0.5 FTE — against the top 50 accounts.
- **Reprice for value.** $49–89/user is priced like a CRM while delivering methodology infrastructure that costs $165+ to approximate on Salesforce. Options: raise the top tier meaningfully; add a platform fee (which also monetizes the API/agent access from §5); value-price Elevate against training budgets (which are 10x software budgets) rather than software budgets.
- **Rescue the at-risk segment.** The customers using a sliver of the platform are the churn/build-your-own risk. A low-touch "full-value onboarding" replay for the bottom quartile of usage is cheaper than replacing them.
- **Cost side.** 28 people across three countries serving 200 customers needs an honest zero-based review with Henrik. Profitability within 12–18 months should be a stated, non-negotiable goal — it converts every later choice (including Path C) from forced to optional.

1. **Unit-economics truth pack** — NRR, churn, CAC by channel, module penetration, usage quartiles. One page. (Weeks 1–4)
2. **Win/loss on the last 50 decisions** (won, lost, churned) — especially: what did the build-your-own customers actually build, and why? (Weeks 1–6)

4. **Partner triage** — rank all 119 partners by referrals in the last 24 months; invite the top ~10–15 into the certified tier (partner console + marketplace terms), move the rest to the no-touch community tier, and reallocate the freed partner-management capacity to the direct/community motion. (Weeks 4–12)
5. **AI roadmap decision** — commit to coach + methodology encoding toolkit + API/MCP + benchmarks; explicitly kill generic-AI feature work. Prototype the encoding toolkit with one certified partner's actual playbooks as the first proof. (Weeks 4–8)
6. **Pricing test** — new top tier / platform fee on the next 20 deals and renewals. (Weeks 6–12)
7. **Owner alignment** — you, Henrik, key employees, angels agree on Path A and the profitability deadline, in writing. (Week 1 — this is actually first.)

## 8. Risks and honest counterpoints

- **The certified tier may still disappoint.** Fourteen years of partner effort produced 119 mostly-dormant relationships; the concentrated bet could fail the same way if selection is by enthusiasm rather than evidence. Gate it hard: invitation requires demonstrated referrals or clients already migrating onto Membrain, and the tier gets 12 months to source real pipeline before its investment is re-examined. The direct motion must never depend on it.
- **Partner-console build cost.** A multi-client console is a real product investment competing with the AI roadmap for the same small team. Sequence: validate with 2–3 design partners on manual workarounds before committing engineering. (The encoding toolkit is different — it *is* the AI roadmap, shared between the coach and the partner motion, so it doesn't compete for a separate budget.)
- **Editions déjà vu.** The encoding toolkit could fail like the methodology editions did if it's framed as a demand-gen SKU. The guardrails: no cold landing pages, no per-methodology engineering, distribution only through live engagements, and the toolkit's success metric is *activation and stickiness of accounts that used it* — not leads generated.
- **Repricing churn.** Raising prices on a not-yet-sticky segment can accelerate the exact churn you fear. Sequence: fix activation for the low-usage quartile *before* their renewals reprice.
- **Founder gravity.** A strategy leaning on your brand must not recreate founder-dependence. The community and benchmark assets must belong to Membrain, with your voice as launch fuel.
- **The uncomfortable scenario.** If NRR is well below 100% and the win/loss work says the niche is shrinking under AI, Path A's answer changes from "grow" to "harvest profitably and pursue Path C sooner." Better to learn that in Q3 2026 than after another unprofitable year.
